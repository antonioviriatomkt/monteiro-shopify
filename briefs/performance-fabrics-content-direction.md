# Performance Fabrics — Content Direction (Technical Hub + White Paper)

**Purpose:** a structure and first-pass content proposal for the six Performance Fabrics pieces, to be
**reviewed and enriched by a Monteiro specialist** (commercial / technical support / R&D) before writing.

**How to use this doc**
- Each piece has: *who's searching · target queries · the real questions to answer · a section outline ·
  a first-pass angle · 🔬 **Ask the specialist** prompts · the unique-value hook · internal links.*
- The 🔬 prompts are the point. Google no longer rewards keyword injection — it rewards **helpful,
  first-hand, hard-to-find content (helpful-content / E-E-A-T)**. The parts a competitor can't copy from
  a spec sheet are what make these rank: real test values, Monteiro's own cleaning/fire data, and the
  questions leads actually ask. Everything a specialist marks as "proprietary / from experience" is gold.
- **Tactic (per Ivana):** before the specialist writes, ask 2–3 client-facing people (sales, technical
  support) one question each — *"What do customers most often ask you about [fire ratings / cleaning /
  healthcare specs]?"* Their answers become the FAQ and the sub-headings. Content that answers real lead
  questions outperforms everything else.

**Status:** the 5 articles already exist as **drafts** in the Technical Hub blog (`/blogs/technical-hub`)
with placeholder bodies; this direction expands them. The white paper page/PDF is not built yet.

**E-E-A-T levers to pull in every piece**
- **Experience** — Monteiro has made these fabrics for decades; use first-hand phrasing ("in our
  experience", "we test for…", "a common mistake we see…").
- **Expertise** — name the standards precisely; the specialist adds the nuance.
- **Authoritativeness** — cite Monteiro's own certificates, test reports and data (with numbers).
- **Trust** — no fluff, no overclaiming; where a value depends on the product, say so.

## Article 1 — Crib 5 vs Crib 7: which flammability rating do you need?

- **Who's searching:** a specifier / procurement / upholsterer deciding which fire rating a contract
  project requires. High intent, close to a purchase decision.
- **Target queries:** "crib 5 vs crib 7", "difference between crib 5 and crib 7", "which crib rating do I
  need", "bs 5852 crib 5 meaning", "is crib 5 enough for [hospital/stadium]".
- **Real questions to answer:** What's the actual difference? Which do I need for *my* environment? Is
  Crib 5 ever not enough? Does the filling/interliner change the requirement? Cost/lead-time difference?

**Outline**
1. The short answer (2–3 lines, so it can win a featured snippet / AI answer).
2. What BS 5852 actually tests (ignition sources, why "crib" = a bigger source).
3. Crib 5 — the contract benchmark (where it's specified, typical environments).
4. Crib 7 — the highest-hazard rating (where it's required, who mandates it).
5. **Decision guide** — a table/flowchart: environment → rating usually required.
6. Common mistakes (over-specifying, testing the fabric alone vs the composite).
7. Monteiro's certified grades + how to get the certificate.

**First-pass angle:** neutral, decision-focused; leads with the plain-English difference, then a
practical "which one for which environment" table. (Draft body already started in the article.)

🔬 **Ask the specialist**
- Which Monteiro grades are certified to Crib 5 and which to Crib 7? Certificate numbers / test houses?
- Real examples (anonymised) of projects that *required* Crib 7 and why.
- Does the fabric's certification depend on the filling/interliner used? What do you tell customers?
- The most common misunderstanding you correct when a customer asks about crib ratings.
- UK vs mainland-EU reality: when does a client ask for crib vs an EN/DIN class?

**Unique-value hook:** a genuinely useful *"which crib rating for which environment"* decision table +
real (anonymised) project examples — neither is on competitors' pages.
**Internal links:** → Performance Fabrics pillar, → CHANCE 2.0 fire grades (Crib 5/Crib 7 lines), Article 5 (B1).

## Article 2 — How to choose fabrics for healthcare environments

- **Who's searching:** healthcare procurement / furniture manufacturers / interior specifiers choosing
  upholstery for clinical settings.
- **Target queries:** "how to choose healthcare upholstery fabric", "best fabric for hospital furniture",
  "antimicrobial upholstery fabric", "medical grade fabric requirements".
- **Real questions:** What criteria actually matter? How do I compare options? Which certs are non-negotiable?
  Will it survive our cleaning protocol?

**Outline**
1. Short answer / the 5 things that decide it.
2. Hygiene & infection control (non-absorbent, antimicrobial).
3. Cleanability (which disinfectants — the make-or-break).
4. Fire safety (link to Article 1).
5. Durability (Martindale — what number for what use).
6. Compliance & chemical safety (REACH, phthalate-free).
7. Comfort & patient experience.
8. **Specification checklist** (downloadable).

**First-pass angle:** a practical buyer's decision guide, ending in a checklist they can reuse.

🔬 **Ask the specialist**
- Which disinfectants are our healthcare fabrics validated against — and at what **concentration and
  contact time**? (This is the single most valuable, hardest-to-find detail.)
- What Martindale figure do we quote for healthcare lines, and what's "enough" for patient seating vs
  high-turnover chairs?
- Which antimicrobial standard/test do we hold (e.g. ISO 22196 / ISO 20743) and the result?
- The top 3 specification mistakes you see procurement teams make.
- Any real question a hospital buyer asked that surprised you — it belongs in the FAQ.

**Unique-value hook:** Monteiro's **validated cleaning matrix** (which disinfectant, what dilution, how
long) + a reusable spec checklist. Procurement teams bookmark this kind of thing.
**Internal links:** → /collections/healthcare, → /collections/medical, → Article 4 (bleach), pillar.

- Any standard specific to medical furniture we should cite that buyers ask about?

**Unique-value hook:** a *requirements-by-furniture-type* matrix — practical, and rarely done well online.

## Article 4 — Bleach cleaning: what to look for in a fabric

- **Who's searching:** facilities / infection-control / specifiers worried disinfection will wreck the fabric.
- **Target queries:** "bleach cleanable upholstery fabric", "can you bleach vinyl upholstery", "sodium
  hypochlorite fabric damage", "disinfect upholstery without damaging".
- **Real questions:** Will bleach ruin it? What concentration is safe? How do I clean it properly? Which
  fabrics actually survive repeated bleach?

**Outline**
1. Short answer (wipe-clean ≠ bleach-cleanable).
2. Why bleach is so aggressive on fabric.
3. What makes a fabric genuinely bleach-cleanable (colour-stable chemistry, flexible coating, non-absorbent).
4. How to verify before you specify (ask for concentration + contact time in writing).
5. How to clean without shortening fabric life (method, dilution, rinse).
6. Monteiro's cleaning guidance.

**First-pass angle:** a myth-buster + practical how-to; positions Monteiro as the honest expert.

🔬 **Ask the specialist**
- **The exact bleach (sodium hypochlorite) concentration and contact time our fabrics tolerate**, and the
  test evidence behind it. (Highest-value, most-searched, least-available fact on the whole hub.)
- What actually happens to a non-suitable fabric (cracking, colour loss) — describe from experience.
- The cleaning mistakes that void warranties.
- Any real facility cleaning protocol we've advised on (anonymised).

**Unique-value hook:** Monteiro's **actual bleach concentration + contact-time spec** — this single number
set, stated plainly, could own the query.
**Internal links:** → Article 2, → /collections/healthcare, → /collections/medical, pillar.

## Article 5 — Understanding B1 certification

- **Who's searching:** specifiers on German/EU or transport/public-building projects who hit a "B1"
  requirement and need to understand it.
- **Target queries:** "what is b1 certification", "din 4102 b1 meaning", "b1 fire rating fabric", "b1 vs
  euroclass", "b1 vs crib 5".
- **Real questions:** What is B1? Where is it required? How does it relate to EN 13501 / BS 5852? Does
  *this* fabric hold it?

**Outline**
1. Short answer (B1 = flame-retardant under DIN 4102-1).
2. What B1 means (the DIN scale, B1 vs B2).
3. Where B1 is required (German/EU projects, transport, public buildings).
4. **Cross-standard comparison** — B1 vs EN 13501-1 Euroclasses vs BS 5852 crib (a table).
5. How to verify (ask for the certificate, check it covers the product form).
6. Monteiro's B1 grades.

**First-pass angle:** the clear explainer that also disambiguates B1 from the other standards buyers confuse it with.

🔬 **Ask the specialist**
- Which Monteiro fabrics hold B1, with certificate numbers / test house?
- The correct cross-references (is our B1 also classifiable under EN 13501-1? which Euroclass?).
- German-market specifics — what do DACH clients actually ask for?
- The most common B1 misconception.

**Unique-value hook:** a **cross-standard comparison table** (B1 ↔ Euroclass ↔ crib) — high utility, hard
to find in one place — plus Monteiro's verified B1 certs.
**Internal links:** → Article 1 (crib), → Performance Fabrics pillar, → CHANCE 2.0 Crib 7 / B1 grade.

## White paper — The Complete Guide to Healthcare Upholstery Fabrics

- **Role:** the **pillar asset / gated lead magnet**. The five articles are spokes that link up into this.
  Format: a designed PDF (and/or a long-form gated page). Gate behind an email capture → the Performance
  Fabrics inbox Ivana is setting up.
- **Who's downloading:** healthcare procurement, furniture OEMs, A&D specifiers who want one authoritative
  reference to keep.
- **Why it earns links/citations:** a genuinely useful *specification toolkit* (checklist + glossary +
  cleaning matrix + cert reference) is the kind of asset people save, share and cite — the strongest E-E-A-T signal.

**Proposed chapters**
1. Why healthcare upholstery is a technical decision (the risk framing).
2. The requirements, in depth (hygiene, fire, durability, compliance, comfort) — expands Articles 2 & 3.
3. Certifications explained (antimicrobial standards, fire — crib & B1, REACH, OEKO-TEX, PFAS) — pulls in Articles 1 & 5.
4. Cleaning & disinfection (the bleach/alcohol matrix) — expands Article 4.
5. How to choose & specify — the decision process.
6. **Toolkit (the reusable bit):** a specification **checklist**, a **cleaning/disinfectant matrix**, a
   **glossary** of standards, and a one-page **cert reference**.
7. About Monteiro (experience & capability — the E-E-A-T "who says so").

🔬 **Ask the specialist**
- Everything from Articles 1–5, consolidated — this is where the deep, proprietary detail lives.
- A real specification template Monteiro uses/recommends.
- The full cleaning/disinfectant matrix across the healthcare range.
- Any Monteiro test data / LCA / project outcomes we can publish (with permission).

**Unique-value hook:** the **toolkit** (checklist + matrix + glossary + cert reference). That's the
downloadable people keep.
**Internal links / flow:** every article ends with "for the full picture, download the Guide"; the Guide's
CTA → Get in touch. The Technical Hub section on the Performance Fabrics page already features it.
